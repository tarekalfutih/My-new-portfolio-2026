02The challenge

Planning a trip means checking many sources

Anglers often rely on multiple apps and sources to check fishing locations, weather, wind, water conditions, and regulations. Smart Fishing brings this information together to make planning a fishing trip simpler and more informed.

03Journey 1

Where and when should I fish?

Smart Fishing brings location, weather, wind, water level, waves, and other marine conditions together to help anglers understand where and when conditions may be suitable for fishing.

Smart Fishing does not detect where fish are. Instead, it uses fishing locations together with current weather and marine conditions to estimate how suitable the conditions are for fishing.

The app looks at information such as water temperature, wind, sea level, and waves, then applies different rules depending on the fish species. The result is simplified into three levels — Excellent, Good, Fair — so the user doesn't have to analyze all the raw data separately.

How the score is calculated
Fishing location→ Environmental conditions→ Species-specific rules→ Fishing potential

Each fishing location is tied to a species, and I check that species against live wind, water temperature, sea level, and wave height data from Open-Meteo.

Where it's headed

The recommendation is still a rule-based estimate and needs further development and testing. It doesn't yet learn from catch records, or use season, seabed depth, or real fish observations.

I've also found some technical issues in the current version that I'm planning to fix.

04Journey 2 — Record a catch

More than just a photo

User testing showed that anglers wanted to document more about their catches. The redesigned record lets users save and share details such as species, length, weight, bait or lure, location, and photos.

05Learn

Knowing how to fish, not just where

Beyond location and conditions, Smart Fishing includes a Learn section covering topics such as weather and water temperature, tides, rods and reels, lures, live bait, knots, float fishing, fishing ethics, and fish species — so anglers can build fishing knowledge directly in the app.

02 — Catch records lacked detail
Before
After

Users wanted to share more information about their catches.

Problem: The original record was too limited.

Response: Added species, length, weight, bait/lure, location, photos, and catch details.

Implemented — needs further user testing
03 — Forecast lacked fishing guidance
Before
After

A standard weather forecast did not clearly tell users whether it was a good fishing day or when to fish.

Problem: Users had to interpret weather data themselves.

Response: Redesigned the forecast around fishing suitability and suitable fishing times.

Implemented — needs further user testing
04 — More water information
New

More detailed water conditions were needed.

Problem: The experience lacked useful marine context.

Response: Added water level and trends, wave height, water temperature, and other marine conditions.

Implemented — needs further user testing
07Product

From prototype to product

After testing and redesigning the Figma prototype, I moved the concept into a functional mobile app to explore the experience with real data and interactions.

I used Flutter/Dart for the application and Codex as an AI-assisted development tool, while maintaining ownership of the UX, interaction design, requirements, review, and refinement. Methods throughout: design iteration, usability testing, and user interviews.

09Reflection

What I learned

Smart Fishing taught me how to take an idea past Figma and into a real product, through testing, iteration, and building it. I learned Material Design 3 and worked with AI-assisted development in Codex to turn my UX and interaction design decisions into a working app.

It also taught me a lot about how UX, real data, APIs, and technical limits all affect each other in a real product.
