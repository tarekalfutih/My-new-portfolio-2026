01 · Game description

The premise

In Color Rotation, players navigate a rotating circular platform loaded with colourful tiles. Their goal is to collect colour blocks and complete challenging sequences to earn points, all while competing with each other. The race is on, with each turn bringing new obstacles and challenges as the board shifts beneath them.

Theme & setting
Colours, rotation, sequence alignment.
Target audience
Players with an interest in strategy and puzzle-based games.
Goal
Complete the quests and gain enough points to win.
02 · How it plays

Setup & the core loop

To set up, players randomly place the colour tiles across the board's three rotatable floors, take a player mat, and draw one easy, one medium and one hard sequence card. Each turn, a player rotates the floor they're standing on and moves to a neighbouring tile — on the same floor or a different one — to collect its colour block.

Components

  • 4 playing pieces
  • 1 board with rotating layers
  • 28 coloured tiles
  • 120 resources (20 × 6)
  • 4 playing mats
  • 12 tokens
  • 8 special cards
  • 36 quest cards (12 × 3)
Refined through playtesting: Steal, Take an extra quest, Swap a card, See another player's cards, Discard 2 resources, Duplicate a resource, Swap adjacent.

Mechanics

Move, rotate, collect resources
  • Move to adjacent tiles
  • Collect resources from tiles
  • Rotate the layer
Resources & sequences
  • Resources are stored as a sequence, maximum 5 slots
  • A newly gained resource can be placed on the left or the right of the current sequence
  • If the queue is already full, the block furthest from the side it was added to gets removed
Tokens & special cards
  • Players can take a token when skipping their turn
  • Tokens can be exchanged for special cards
  • The original design's four card types: stealing from another player, swapping with another player, receiving a bonus block already owned, or moving a player back to the initial position

Turn structure

Each player has 2 action points per turn.
  • 2 points — take a token
  • 1 point — move to an adjacent tile, rotate a layer 90°, or remove a resource
  • 0 points — spend tokens, or complete a quest
7

Winning

Complete quests with sequences to gain points. 7 points to win.
03 · Positioning

Where it sits in the market

Competitors

  • Resource management: Catan, Splendor
  • Complete the sequence: Azul, Sagrada
  • Gathering colours: Hexa Sort, Rainbow Balls, Candy Crush
  • Interactive board: Centrix, Hungry Turtle
Closest in spirit to Azul, where players also build sequences to score — though here resources come from moving around the board and slot into a limited, rearrangeable queue while other players can interfere. Catan shares the resources-to-score loop, but its builds only need a quantity of resources, not an exact order.

Unique selling points

  • Colourful game components
  • Interactive, reconfigurable game board
  • Strategic gameplay with a puzzle element
  • Diverse strategies and replayability
04 · Design process

Two stages, Jones' model reapplied

Conceptualization first, then prototyping — each carried through its own cycle of brainstorm, refine, choose.

Conceptualization
  • Brainstorming, discussing different ideas, identifying promising ideas
  • Creating different game suggestions from the identified ideas, refining them, choosing the most promising suggestion
Prototyping
  • Brainstorming and testing different constructions, continuing on the best versions
  • Identifying issues through playtests, testing ways to remedy them, choosing the best remedy

Playtesting — 9 sessions over 3 days

1
Day 1 — 5/12/24
  • Session 1: first impressions on mechanics — no objective or special cards.
  • Session 2: resource shifting and quest mechanics.
  • Session 3: rotation and shifting feedback, altered instructions, no quests or special cards.
  • Session 4: basic actions with quests included, altered instructions.
2
Day 2 — 6/12/24
  • Session 5: special cards, given out free every 2 turns.
  • Session 6: quest timing across difficulties — players had to finish quests with increasing difficulty.
  • Session 7: quest balance, 2-player — one player only played hard quests, one only easy and medium.
3
Day 3 — 10/12/24
  • Session 8: full 4-player game, testing overall length.
  • Session 9: co-op setup — 4 players in 2 teams, each sharing a mat, tokens and quests.
05 · Iteration

From first concept to current prototype

Changes since the first concept

Action point system revamp
  • Changed from set actions taken each turn to a points-based system
  • More possible player actions
Prototype board refinement
  • Coloured board tiles
  • Double coding on some game elements
  • An action point cheat sheet
Special card & token refinement
  • Altered special cards and their costs
Win condition
  • Dropped from 12 points in the first concept to 7 points in the current prototype

Next steps

06 · What we learned

Takeaways from nine playtests

Main takeaways

  • Keep the prototype simple and easy to adjust before adding complexity.
  • Designing a game is an iterative process — start iterating.
  • Experiment with ideas outside your comfort zone.
  • Listen to feedback from players with diverse playstyles and experiences.
  • Understand how game mechanisms shape different experiences and interactions.
  • Focus on specific audiences; you can't please everyone.
  • Making a game fun is a challenge.

How the team worked together

Making the game
  • Everyone contributed ideas during the planning phase
  • We designed and tested the game together, with active participation from all
During playtesting
  • We built the prototype and split into groups for testing sessions
  • We rotated roles during sessions, making it a fully collaborative effort
