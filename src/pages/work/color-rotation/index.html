01Overview

What is it?

Color Rotation is a competitive board game about movement, colour and strategy.

Players move across a rotating board, collect Colour Blocks and arrange them in the right order to complete Quests.

The objective
Collect colours. Complete quests. Reach 7 points first.
Players
2 to 4
Who it is for
Designed for players who enjoy strategy and puzzle-based board games.

03Positioning

What makes it different

Similar games

Resource managementCatan · SplendorSequence buildingAzul · SagradaColour collectionHexa Sort · Rainbow Balls · Candy CrushInteractive boardCentrix · Hungry Turtle
Closest in spirit: Azul

Both games involve collecting pieces and building sequences to score.

Color Rotation adds movement across a changing board, a limited 5-block sequence and direct player interaction.

How Color Rotation stands out

↻
A board that keeps changing

Rotating layers change available paths.

→→
Order matters

Players need the right colours in the right order.

5 / 5

⇄
Players affect each other

Board rotation and Special Cards can disrupt another player’s plan.

04Design process

I contributed throughout the full design process — from early ideas and prototyping to playtesting and iteration — as part of a collaborative team.

  • Everyone contributed ideas during planning.

05Iteration

What we saw, and what we changed

Playtesting shaped the game. These are the main changes from the first concept to the final prototype.

Turns needed more flexibility

Before

Players took a fixed set of actions every turn.

→
After

A 2-Action-Point system. Players choose how to spend each turn, with more actions to choose from.

A shorter win condition

Before · first concept
12points to win
→
After · final prototype
7points to win

Clearer guidance on the prototype

  • Coloured board tilesEach tile shows the colour of the Colour Block you collect there.
  • Double codingWe added another visual cue alongside colour on some game elements. For example, each tile shows a letter as well as a colour: B for blue, G for green. This use of double coding reduced reliance on colour alone.
  • Action Point referenceA cheat sheet showing what each action costs.

Special Cards changed through testing

First version · 4 card types
  • Steal from another player
  • Swap with another player
  • Get a bonus Colour Block you already own
  • Send another player back to start
→
After playtesting · 7 cards
  • Steal
  • Take an extra Quest
  • Swap a card
  • See another player’s cards
  • Discard 2 Colour Blocks
  • Duplicate a Colour Block
  • Swap adjacent

We also changed what the cards cost.

07Reflection

What we learned

Start simple

Early prototypes should be easy to change.

Test early

Playtesting revealed problems we could not see while designing.

Different players reveal different problems

Different play styles gave us different perspectives.

Mechanics shape behaviour

Small rule changes changed how players planned and interacted.

Design for a clear audience

Trying to satisfy everyone can weaken the experience.

Fun needs testing too

A game can be understandable without being enjoyable. Both need testing.
