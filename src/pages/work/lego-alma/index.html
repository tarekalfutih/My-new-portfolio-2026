01Problem & brief

An installation that needed a guide

LEGO ALMA is a LEGO model of the ALMA radio telescope at Onsala Space Observatory, built to demonstrate radio interferometry. Its original design relied on a guide standing beside it to explain the unintuitive on-screen graphs, so visitors exploring on their own — especially the 12–18 age group the center wanted to reach — struggled to understand what it was showing or what to do.

The brief: redesign the installation so a broader, unsupervised audience could use it on its own, without losing the complexity of what it teaches.

Research question

What are key factors to consider when improving accessibility and engagement in a tangible user interface aimed at science communication?

03Iteration — Research through Design, three rounds

Learning by building and testing

Research through Design was used because the research question could not be answered through theory alone. We needed to design and test the installation to understand what worked in practice.

An iterative process helped us improve the design step by step. We developed low-, mid-, and high-fidelity prototypes, tested them with users, and used what we learned from each round to guide the next design.

Low-fidelity

Paper-and-wood model plus a wireframe screen, tested with six students and an external mentor. Reducing visual complexity increased curiosity, but the antenna-placement instructions were easy to miss.

Mid-fidelity · 4 rounds

A Figma-built UI run on the real installation, tested with children across four iterations. Removed the reference image and antenna map to keep curiosity up, and added an antenna count and placement animation for clearer feedback.

High-fidelity

The final interface, built in Python and observed unassisted over three sessions with 9th graders. A background and text were added to the placement animation to make it easier to notice and understand.

05Accessibility decisions

Six factors that made it easier to use

Answer to the research question — six key factors identified across the testing rounds

01

Reduce overload

Short text and animation made complex concepts easier to follow.

02

Audio feedback

Increased engagement and guided attention to digital feedback.

03

Balance resources

Fewer physical elements encouraged exploration.

04

Connect input & output

Same line of sight made interactions easier to understand.

05

Animation with text

Pairing them made concepts land better than either alone.

06

Physical–digital coherency

Complementary action and feedback made cause and effect easier to read.

08Reflection

What stayed unsolved

Two things stayed unsolved: the physical buttons sit too far from most visitors' hands to reach comfortably, and users have to keep moving their heads between the board and the screen, missing feedback in between. Worth testing next: buttons set flat on the board, and a screen positioned lower and closer.
